Section 03

Biosecurity Zones & Measures

Controlled Access Zone (CAZ)

Separates the farm from the external environment — the first checkpoint for people, vehicles and materials.

Restricted Access Zone (RAZ)

Core areas such as the poultry houses themselves — access limited to essential, PPE-equipped staff.

Emergency Preparedness

  • Early detection of diseases
  • Quarantine procedures
  • Reporting to veterinary authorities
  • A documented action plan for disease outbreaks

The Disease Triangle

Disease emerges only when Host, Pathogen and Environment intersect. Bio-security works by breaking that intersection — remaining the cheapest and most effective means of disease control available.

Host Pathogen (agent) Environment — birds, food, water, soil, insects, equipment, people

Section 05

Site Selection — The Land

  • Elevated land with good drainage facility
  • Open space & good air ventilation
  • Presence of underground drinking water & sufficient sunlight
  • Good road facility for easy transportation
  • Water quality: hardness < 250 ppm, pH 6.0–7.0
  • Availability of electricity
  • Easy availability of manpower

Section 10

Cleaning & Disinfection — The Basics

Cleaning

Removal of unwanted material ("soils") from production equipment and production areas.

Sanitizing

Treatment of a clean surface with a chemical or physical agent (e.g. heat) to reduce microorganisms to safe levels.

Disinfection

Reduces the number of organisms present to a level where they no longer threaten health — usually does not kill spores.

Sterilisation

Scientific: kills all organism types, probability only 1 remains from a starting count of 1,000,000,000.
Practical: kills all organisms present, including spores.

Biofilm — The Hidden Threat

Incomplete cleaning, sanitation or disinfection encourages biofilm to develop — a protective mass that traps nutrients and bacteria, becoming an ongoing source of contamination from surfaces that look clean. Both pathogenic and spoilage microbes can form a biofilm, and it must be physically removed before disinfection will work.

Bacterial attachment → protective slime film → traps nutrients & bacteria. This prevents anti-microbial action — effective cleaning is required first.
Section 11

What Affects Disinfection Efficacy

Concentration

Use the recommended concentration. Too high → wasted cost. Too low → resistant strains of microorganisms develop.

Formulation

Two products with identical active-ingredient concentration can still differ in efficacy due to other formulation components.

Contact time

No disinfectant works instantaneously. The validated contact time is on the product label — if it needs more than 30 minutes, it's a weak disinfectant.

Temperature

Higher temperature increases disinfectant efficacy and colloidal movement. Note: iodine/chlorine/iodophor become ineffective above 46°C.

Relative humidity

Affects penetration into dried material. Formalin, for example, needs RH ≈ 70% to be efficient.

Application method

Amount delivered to the target depends on method — apply by gentle spraying to increase contact time.

Biofilm presence

Must be removed before disinfection — otherwise it shields organisms from the disinfectant entirely.

Pre-cleaning

A critical step for efficacy. The pre-cleaner must be compatible with the disinfectant, applied with high-pressure hot water (≥ 40 psi).

Target Organism Resistance (low → high)

Pathogens carry different degrees of resistance to germicides:

1. Bacterial spores 2. Non-enveloped viruses (IBD, chicken anemia) 3. Mycobacteria 4. Fungi 5. Enveloped viruses (AI, NDV) 6. Vegetative bacteria

Never Mix These Disinfectants

Chlorine + peroxide → loses activity. Iodophores + soaps → loses activity. Give each disinfectant its own contact time, then allow 1 day of rest before applying another.

PurposeQuantity

Criteria of the Ideal Disinfectant

Effective disinfectant is:

  • A powerful poison
  • Toxic

Safe disinfectant is:

  • Non-poisonous
  • Relatively poor at killing micro-organisms

The tension between the two is exactly why concentration, contact time and application method all matter so much in practice.

Section 12

Shed Cleaning Process

Shed cleaning combines dry cleaning and wet cleaning into one complete turnaround protocol between flocks.

Pre-cleaning procedures

Allow a 2-week down time for clean-up and preparation for the next flock. Keep the electric current supply off before beginning the cleaning process.

Dry washing

After bird lifting, empty feeders and disconnect drinkers, removing them outside the shed — litter remains as usual for now.

Pre-spray

Using a low-pressure sprayer, spray a detergent solution from ceiling to floor to dampen down dust before litter and equipment are removed. Close curtains first in open-sided houses.

Top-to-bottom sweep

Clean side walls, wire net, ceiling, beams and supports with a broomstick and hard brush so all cobwebs, dust and dirt fall onto the litter before removal. Always clean top to bottom.

Litter treatment & removal

Scrape litter into small heaps, spray with a good terminal disinfectant, and leave for 24 hours. Then remove old litter and manure completely for sale, or dispose at least 500 ft from the farm, opposite the wind direction. Collect and burn feathers and other waste in and around the farm.

Flame gun treatment

Use direct flame for pathogen elimination, especially after heavy mortality or infection in the previous batch. The intense heat sanitizes floors, walls and equipment, reaching nooks and crannies manual cleaning misses.

Repair & floor renewal

Watch for holes, cracks and crevices and repair them. For soiled floors, remove the top ½ inch of soil and replace with new soil, cow dung and red soil (where available) to prepare a plain floor area.

Insecticide spray

While the house is still warm after bird removal, spray equipment and all surfaces with insecticide so pests have no chance to migrate.

Wet washing

Jet spray of water across all surfaces to complete the physical clean before disinfection begins.

White wash / painting

Apply to all sidewalls, floors, pillars and outside surfaces.

Formula for 500 sq ft: Liquid lime 1.5 kg + Bleaching powder 250 g + Water 5 L + Copper sulphate 100 g + Kerosene 150 ml → rest 24 hours → next day add 250 ml formalin → followed by white wash.

Dry & close

Dry the shed in sunlight, then keep it closed with curtains on both sides.

Cleaning Individual Systems

🥤 Equipment

Use detergents throughout. Remove scale in drinkers with 31% HCl. Remove all fittings — drinkers, feeders, fences — to the external concrete area; raise automatic feeders and nipple drinkers during cleaning, then leave exposed to sun.

🛢️ Overhead tank

Scrub to remove slime and algae → wash with flowing water → fill with 0.5% bleaching powder solution and hold overnight → flush with plain water.

🚿 Pipeline

Fill the whole pipeline with 5% H₂O₂, hold for 6 hours, then flush with fresh water — this clears biofilm blockages along the drink system.

🎪 Curtains

Day 1: thorough clean with plain water. Day 2: soak in 2% formalin water for 12–24 hours. Then sun-dry.

🌾 Feeding system

Scrape and scrub to completely remove old feed. Remove the feed-bin boot and clean out remaining feed. Follow a soaking → foaming → rinsing sequence.

Prevention of Coccidia

Homogenous mixture of common salt, or caustic soda flakes: 1–2 kg caustic soda + 30 L water per 1,000 sq ft.

Aerial Disinfection Methods

1. Spraying

Knapsack sprayer or pressure washer — relatively large particles, higher wetting capacity, but stay airborne for less time. Any good commercial disinfectant, 24 hours before chick placement, e.g. Viruquat 3 ml/1 L water.

2. Fumigation

Combining 2+ chemicals to produce a vaporised disinfectant — mainly used with formaldehyde. For 1,000 sq ft, 3 days before chick arrival: KMnO₄ 600 g + Formalin 1,200 ml.

3. Thermal fogging

Heating the disinfectant to produce a fine vapor. Small particle size gives the best penetration and suspension.

Bio-security Checklist

  • Proper fencing
  • Vehicle spray
  • Separate clothing
  • Foot dip at entrance
  • Minimum plantation
  • Proper water sanitization
  • Separate PM room
  • Proper disposal of dead birds
  • Minimum visitors
  • Entry restricted to stray/pet animals
  • No exchange of material from neighbouring farms
  • All-in, all-out system
Keep gate closed — no matter what the chickens say. Bio-security is only as strong as its most casually left-open door.
